## What the numbers mean

- 4 of the 9 registries this run reached (quay.io, public.ecr.aws, registry.k8s.io and codeberg.org) do not honour the cheap conditional re-ask PLAN_90 relies on — asking "has this changed?" saves nothing at those registries, and a full digest fetch is paid every pass regardless.
- 8 of 9 registries (ghcr.io, lscr.io, quay.io, public.ecr.aws, mcr.microsoft.com, registry.k8s.io, codeberg.org and registry.gitlab.com) send no allowance headers at all, so a future wallet feature has to keep its own tally for them rather than reading one back from the registry.
- mcr.microsoft.com needs no token at all — the open-registry branch’s only live case among the nine.
- public.ecr.aws names no digest in its reply, which is why the computed-from-the-bytes fallback exists at all: without it those registries would report no digest, not a wrong one.
- registry.gitlab.com names its token realm on a different host entirely — proof the realm the challenge names has to be followed wherever it points, rather than assumed to sit on the registry’s own host.

## Where a quirk needed code

- **The placeholder-scope fix in `staxx_registry_token()`** — ghcr.io and codeberg.org answer `/v2/` with a placeholder repository scope in the challenge itself; trusting it instead of building the scope from the repository actually being asked about breaks every image on the affected host. Proven again by this run.
- **The computed-digest fallback in `staxx_registry_digest()`** — a registry that names no digest header gets one computed from the exact bytes served, rather than being reported as having no digest at all.
- **The plain-HTTP transport opt-in in `staxx_registry_trusted()` / `staxx_registry_challenge()`** — a self-hosted registry with no certificate is only reachable at all once its host is named in `REGISTRY_TRUST`; the hostile-redirect guard on the challenge’s realm is unchanged either way.
